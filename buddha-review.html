The most important contrast: Traditional biography says Siddhartha left after seeing age, sickness, death, and a renunciant. Ambedkar emphasizes his principled opposition to a proposed war over the Rohini River. Divyakirti discusses both; neither should be confused with a conclusively established eyewitness record.

Five ideas you should be able to recall

1 · Historical context

Iron agriculture → surplus → towns and trade → powerful states → challenges to ritual and social hierarchy.

2 · The Middle Way

Neither indulgence nor severe self-mortification reliably leads to liberation.

3 · The Four Noble Truths

Dukkha · origin · cessation · path. A diagnosis of suffering, not necessarily “nothing good exists.”

4 · Equal spiritual potential

Upali’s ordination, Mahaprajapati’s persistence, and Ananda’s question about women test inherited status barriers.

5 · Dhamma before personality

The teaching and disciplined inquiry—not absolute obedience to a successor—are the lasting guide.

Sayings and expressions worth remembering

Standard spellings are supplied in romanization and Devanagari. “Traditional / canonical expression” means useful Buddhist vocabulary; it does not mean Divyakirti necessarily uttered that exact wording in the recording. Popular sayings may have contested literal attribution.

Refuge formula · Traditional PaliBuddhaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi
बुद्धं शरणं गच्छामि

“I go to the Buddha for refuge.” Followed by Dhammaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi (धम्मं शरणं गच्छामि) and Saṅghaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi (संघं शरणं गच्छामि). Together, the Three Refuges / Three Jewels.

Invitation to ordain · Traditional PaliEhi bhikkhu / Etha bhikkhave
एहि भिक्खु / एथ भिक्खवे

Ehi bhikkhu means “Come, monk” (singular); etha bhikkhave means “Come, monks” (plural). The expression caratha bhikkhave cārikaṃ (“Go forth, monks, on your travels”) is associated with Buddha sending disciples out to teach. Do not conflate these forms.

Self-reliance · Lecture themeAppa dīpo bhava
अप्प दीपो भव

“Be a lamp unto yourself” is a popular rendering. The canonical Pali formulation includes attadīpā viharatha (“dwell with yourselves as an island/refuge”), so this familiar slogan is best treated as a paraphrase rather than a precise verbatim last sentence.

First sermonDhammacakkappavattana
धम्मचक्कप्पवत्तन / धर्मचक्र प्रवर्तन

“Setting the wheel of Dhamma in motion.” Buddha’s first teaching at Sarnath to the five former ascetic companions.

Way of practiceMajjhimā Paṭipadā
मज्झिमा पटिपदा / मध्यम प्रतिपदा

The Middle Way, discovered after rejecting the extremes of sensual indulgence and harsh asceticism.

CausalityPratītyasamutpāda
प्रतीत्यसमुत्पाद

Dependent origination: things arise in dependence on causes and conditions. Pali: paṭiccasamuppāda.

Three characteristicsAnicca · Dukkha · Anattā
अनिच्च · दुक्ख · अनत्ता

Impermanence; dissatisfaction/suffering; no permanent, independent self. Sanskrit equivalents include anitya and anātman.

Four Noble TruthsCattāri ariyasaccāni
चत्तारि अरियसच्चानि / चार आर्य सत्य

Dukkha; its origin; cessation; the path. Core orientation of Buddhist practice.

Path of practiceAriya aṭṭhaṅgika magga
आर्य अष्टांगिक मार्ग

Right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness, and concentration.

Four life stages of the pathSotāpanna · Sakadāgāmī · Anāgāmī · Arahant

Stream-enterer, once-returner, non-returner, and arahant. Ananda’s question about women’s spiritual capacity refers to these attainments.

Passing awayMahāparinibbāna
महापरिनिब्बान / महापरिनिर्वाण

The final passing of an awakened being; traditionally distinguished from achieving nirvana while living.

Interpretive caution“If you meet the Buddha, kill him.”

A later Zen provocation about refusing blind spiritual dependency. Metaphorical, not a direct quotation from the historical Buddha and not advocacy of violence.

People: who did what?

Siddhartha Gautama / Buddha

Shakya-born seeker whose renunciation, awakening, and teachings give Buddhism its starting point.

Shuddhodana · Mahamaya · Mahaprajapati Gautami

Father; birth mother; aunt/foster mother who later seeks admission for women to the Sangha.

Yashodhara · Rahula

Wife and son. Yashodhara is portrayed very differently in Ashvaghosha and Ambedkar. Rahula asks for his inheritance.

Asita · Channa · Kanthaka

Sage who predicts greatness; charioteer of the four-sights story; horse of the Great Departure.

Devadatta · Ananda · Nanda

Rival cousin; loyal cousin and attendant; half-brother who struggles with attachment.

Alara Kalama · Uddaka Ramaputta

Teachers who instruct Siddhartha in meditative attainments that he ultimately regards as insufficient.

Sujata · five ascetics

Woman who offers nourishing food; former companions who hear his first sermon at Sarnath.

Yasa · Assaji

Wealthy young convert; disciple whose explanation deeply impresses Sariputta.

Sariputta · Moggallana

Foremost disciples who previously studied with Sanjaya Belatthiputta.

Upali

Barber ordained before aristocratic Shakya youths; associated with the preservation of monastic discipline.

Sudatta / Anathapindika · Prince Jeta

Merchant patron who funds Jetavana; royal landowner who provides the grove.

Bimbisara · Ajatashatru · Prasenajit

Magadha’s king; his ambitious son; ruler of Kosala. Important political figures across the story.

Uruvela, Nadi and Gaya Kashyapa

Respected ascetic brothers whose communities join the Buddhist movement.

Amrapali · Chunda

Influential woman of Vaishali who hosts Buddha; giver of the final meal before his illness.

Mahavira · Makkhali Gosala · Sanjaya Belatthiputta

Contemporary spiritual teachers representing Jain asceticism, Ajivika determinism, and skepticism.

Ashvaghosha · B. R. Ambedkar

Influential traditional literary biographer and twentieth-century rational-social interpreter whose contrasting biographies frame the lecture.

Places: mental map of the Buddha story

Lumbini · Kapilavastu

Birthplace in tradition; political center associated with Siddhartha’s Shakya upbringing.

Rohini River

Shakya–Koliya irrigation conflict central to Ambedkar’s account of the renunciation.

Rajagriha / Rajgir · Magadha

Political and religious hub associated with King Bimbisara and Buddha’s early search.

Uruvela / Bodh Gaya

Area of austerities and awakening beneath the Bodhi tree.

Sarnath / Deer Park

First sermon to five former ascetics near Varanasi.

Shravasti / Savatthi · Jetavana

Kosala’s major center; monastery established with Anathapindika’s support.

Vaishali

Licchavi confederation; associated with Amrapali and the women’s monastic movement.

Pava · Kushinagar

Meal from Chunda and final illness; Mahaparinirvana among the Mallas.

Books, collections, and schools

Tripitaka / Tipiṭaka · त्रिपिटक

“Three baskets”: Vinaya (discipline), Sutta (discourses), Abhidhamma (analysis). Preserved through long oral transmission before surviving written forms.

Dīgha · Majjhima · Saṃyutta · Aṅguttara · Khuddaka Nikāya

Five Pali discourse collections. Jataka stories are associated with Khuddaka Nikaya; Mahaparinibbana Sutta with Digha Nikaya.

Buddhacharita · बुद्धचरित

Ashvaghosha’s literary biography. Source of the celebrated four-sights story in the lecture’s contrast.

Lalitavistara Sutra · ललितविस्तर सूत्र

Elaborate sacred life narrative associated with Mahayana tradition, including extraordinary and celestial episodes.

The Buddha and His Dhamma · बुद्ध और उनका धम्म

Dr. Ambedkar’s reconstruction, published after his death in 1957; foundational to Navayana interpretations.

Therīgāthā · थेरीगाथा

Early verses attributed to Buddhist nuns, valuable for their first-person spiritual voices.

Theravada · Mahayana · Vajrayana · Navayana

Major streams or later interpretations of Buddhism. They are not interchangeable, and their histories are more complex than a simple linear chain.

Śramaṇa · श्रमण

Renunciant and intellectual movements, including early Buddhism and Jainism, that developed beyond established Vedic sacrificial practice.

Ten rapid-fire questions

Who raised Siddhartha after his mother’s death?

Mahaprajapati Gautami.

What river shaped Ambedkar’s renunciation narrative?

Rohini, disputed by Shakyas and Koliyas.

Where was the awakening?

Bodh Gaya, after he abandoned severe self-denial.

Where was the first sermon?

Sarnath, to five former ascetics.

Who asked Buddha about women’s capacity for liberation?

Ananda.

Who was ordained before the Shakya nobles?

Upali, the barber.

Which merchant founded Jetavana?

Anathapindika (Sudatta), with Prince Jeta’s grove.

Who became Buddha’s two leading disciples?

Sariputta and Moggallana.

Who provided the last meal?

Chunda, at Pava.

Where did Buddha attain Mahaparinirvana?

Kushinagar.
